# Module: course (stub)

Considered, higher-priced education purchases: the Relationship Growth Centre (RGC), later. **This is a stub.** It gets written in full when RGC becomes a launch.

The main source is `../RGC Literature Review/` (a condensed summary; the full review is in Pete's chat history).

## What's different about selling a course

- **A considered, higher-priced purchase.** Buyers take time and compare. Decision support helps them understand, but it doesn't reliably raise purchases (RGC finding 3). So any quiz or selector is a transparent aid, tested against buying directly (FUN-15).
- **Real cohort deadlines only.** Use one real, dated cohort deadline, never a rolling or fake one (LAW-04, RGC finding 7).
- **Judge success on completion and refunds, not clicks.** Unsupported online programmes complete far worse than supported ones: about 6% against 54–66% in relationship-education trials (RGC finding 2). Measure completion and refunds (CUS-10), and keep human support in the programme (CUS-11).
- **Quiz answers may be special category data.** Questions touching health, sex life or relationship circumstances can trigger UK GDPR Article 9 (LAW-16).
- **Planning figures, not targets.** The RGC review summary cites practitioner planning figures, secondhand: waitlist-to-purchase conversion near 2% of a list, with a median lag of about 67 days to first sale (finding 7). Use them to plan cash flow, not as targets. Targets still come from the project's own economics (spec 02).
- **Statistical power.** Course traffic will be small, so test only big changes on high-traffic steps (TRK-07).
- **The enrolment journey.** The RGC review sketches a staged journey for a cohort-taught programme instead of a self-paced course: prospectus, open day, application, advising, offer, acceptance, induction. Worth revisiting when RGC is planned.
