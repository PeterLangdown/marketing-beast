# Extraction notes — Key Person of Influence (Daniel Priestley, 2014 revised ed.)

Structured summary made for personal research, supporting the Guru Vault entry at
`../Guru Vault/data.js` (id: `daniel-priestley`). Not a verbatim copy of the book — short
quotes only, attributed. See the folder README for provenance.

## Premise (Intro, "The World Has Changed")

Careers and loyalty-based employment are framed as obsolete; personal brand is framed as
the only durable source of income security in a digital, global, finely-sliced-into-microniches economy. The book opens with a recurring device — a "hidden theme" the reader is
told they'll only find by re-reading the book, which will supposedly unlock a rush of energy
and total clarity about their future. No further content is attached to this device beyond
the promise itself; it functions as a curiosity/identity hook repeated at intervals through the
book rather than as a teaching tool.

## The KPI Method — five steps, fixed order

States explicitly that the five steps must be done in this order, and that doing them out of
order (e.g. trying products before a credible pitch exists) is why effort fails to produce
results. Each step below is one module in the Guru Vault entry.

**1. Pitch** — a short "social pitch" (answer to "what do you do?", rebuilt around the
listener's payoff) and a longer "presentation pitch" (problem → impact → solution → proof →
ask → uplift) built around a personal "Big Game" (mission). A "polite" reaction signals
failure; the target is a strong, committed reaction.

**2. Publish** — a book (30,000–50,000 words) or a set of 10+ articles, to generate
checkable, Google-able credibility. Five book types offered: thought-leadership, interviews,
tips, picture book, creative/narrative. States plainly that publishing builds credibility while
products generate cash — the book itself is not expected to be a meaningful revenue line.

**3. Product** — a "product ecosystem" split into high-volume (free/cheap: podcasts, kits,
webinars — for relationships and reach) and high-value (productised services — for profit).
Core claim: information has fallen in value because it's freely available; implementation is
what people pay for.

**4. Profile** — deliberate, maintained presence across major platforms (named period-specific
list: YouTube, Slideshare, Facebook, LinkedIn, Wordpress, Twitter, current as of 2014–15),
justified by six stated business objectives (leads, conversion, average spend, purchase
frequency, lower costs, brand). Central claim: "you are who Google says you are."

**5. Partnership** — the "money step." Five deal types (affiliate, co-promotion/list-swap,
product-creation, packaging-up, free-bundle groups). Explicit reframe: network for leverage
(people with a list/product/brand), not for clients. Includes a specific meeting ritual (neutral
venue, small gift, rapport before business, 15–20 minutes of actual deal talk, fast followthrough) and a rule to always confirm agreements in writing.

## Case studies ("KPI Case Studies" chapter)

Six named, detailed case studies, all described as participants in Priestley's own KPI
Program: Mike Symes (financial-services marketing consultant), Paul Fowler (flight-school
owner, "Spitfire Project"), Jane Malyon (English Cream Tea Company, Guinness World
Record stunt), Darren Finkelstein (boat dealer reframed as "boat ownership" business),
Jacqui Sharples (engineer turned fitness entrepreneur, wrote "If Your Body Could Talk"),
and Justine Priestley (the author's sister, founded Really Bright Media). Each case is
narrated as a full walk through all five steps. No outcome data is given for KPI Program
participants beyond these six selected stories.

## Monetisation / promotional structure

Every chapter and several case studies close with a pointer to a paid offshoot: the "Key
Person of Influence Scorecard" (free quiz, used as a lead magnet), the one-day "Brand
Accelerator" event, and the 40-week "KPI Program" (run by Priestley's company, Dent
Global, in cohorts of 40–60 people worldwide, described as including an ILM-certified
element). The final chapters ("The Key Person of Influence Program", "Next Steps") are
explicit sales pages for these offers.

## Headline claim used for the skeptical read

The introduction states readers can become "a Key Person of Influence in your industry
within the next twelve months" if the five steps are followed "at a high standard," without
citing a study, survey or outcome dataset for that specific timeframe — this is the basis for
the "unfalsifiable timeline claim" flagged in the Guru Vault synthesis.
