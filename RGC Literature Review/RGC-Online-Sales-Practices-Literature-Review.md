# Online sales practices for RGC: literature review (summary)

RGC = Relationship Growth Centre, one of the user's own ventures. This is a formal evidence review of online sales/marketing tactics for RGC's funnel, built around a rating taxonomy (Strong/Moderate/Weak/Excluded) and a verdict taxonomy (Adopt/Adapt/Test/Avoid). See `../STAGE-2-VALIDATION-METHODOLOGY-NOTES.md` for the reusable methodology pattern extracted from this review — that note is the main thing to reach for when building Guru Vault's future validation layer.

## Headline position

Honest sales practices (clear all-in prices, real dates, genuine proof, decision support that matches visitors to the right product) are also the best-evidenced ones. Direct evidence for most specific tactics is thin or absent — no controlled study exists of sales practices for paid relationship education, none compares a quiz funnel to a plain landing page, and none tests video sales letters against text. Most recommendations in the review are therefore well-grounded hypotheses to A/B test, not guaranteed lifts. The firmest ground is negative: what UK consumer law bans outright.

## Top-line findings (condensed from the review's own summary table)

1. **Hidden fees, fake urgency, fake reviews and pre-ticked add-ons are banned** under the UK's Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024. The CMA has already fined businesses £4.2m (an omitted £3 fee) and £4.8m (non-expiring "flash deals"). → Show all-in prices, use only real deadlines, publish genuine testimonials, leave add-ons unticked.
2. **Unsupported online programmes complete far worse than coached ones.** In relationship-education trials, unsupported completion ran around 6%, against 54–66% with coaching or tailored emails. → Judge success on completion and refunds, not clicks; protect human touchpoints.
3. **Decision aids improve knowledge and clarity but don't reliably raise purchases** (Cochrane review, 105 trials). → Build any quiz/selector as a transparent aid with answer-linked reasons, and test it against direct buying rather than assuming it converts better.
4. **Quiz answers touching health, sex life or relationship circumstances are likely "special category data"** under UK data law, including inferred data. → Ask the minimum, avoid free text, take separate explicit consent, run a data protection impact assessment.
5. **Design drives first impressions of trust; content and absence of commercial pressure sustain it.** → Keep proof checkable, state credentials exactly, avoid outcome promises.
6. **Price anchoring exists but is weak for willingness-to-pay, and no study shows a premium tier lifts mid-tier sales.** → Don't model an uplift from a top-tier "intensive"; price each product as a genuinely distinct service.
7. **Waitlist-to-purchase conversion in education is essentially unmeasured**; practitioner planning figures cluster near 2% of a list, with a long lag (median ~67 days to first sale). → Plan for low single digits and a long lag; use one real, dated cohort deadline, never a fake one.
8. **Statistical power is a real constraint at low traffic** — detecting a 30% relative lift off a 5% baseline needs ~3,800 visitors per arm. → Test only large, high-traffic steps; use qualitative walkthroughs and sequenced before/after comparisons for everything else.

## Method and rating taxonomy

Full detail in `../STAGE-2-VALIDATION-METHODOLOGY-NOTES.md`. In brief: every finding is tagged Strong / Moderate / Weak / Excluded based on *how much weight the evidence deserves for the specific claim being assessed* — not just whether an effect exists somewhere. A result from a different domain (e.g. chocolate pricing, software trials) is systematically downgraded when applied to a £500 relationship course, even if the underlying study itself is rigorous. "Excluded" isn't a weak rating — it flags tactics that conflict with informed-choice design or UK law outright (fake urgency, invented testimonials, hidden fees, sunk-cost retention tactics, etc.), regardless of whether they'd "work."

Each specific recommendation for the business then gets a separate verdict: **Adopt** (well supported), **Adapt** (keep the tactic but change how it's executed), **Test** (plausible but unproven — needs an experiment), **Avoid** (conflicts with evidence or law).
